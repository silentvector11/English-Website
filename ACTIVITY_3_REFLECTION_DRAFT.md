# Activity 3 — Reflection draft for Avi

*Use this as a starting point. Check the website yourself and change anything that doesn't match your actual experience. The task asks you to reflect on your own choices and challenges.*

## Part A — Design Choices

**1. Overall purpose:** My website explores how social media affects identity and friendships. I wanted people my age to understand that posts only show a part of someone's life, and how the type of content we see and the way we read messages can affect our judgement.

**2. Target audience:** My main audience is teenagers. I used big headlines, short explanations, strong colours and activities like a fake feed and message conversations instead of just making people read a huge wall of text. I also made it work on phones because that is a familiar screen size for the topic.

**3. Proudest design choice:** I am most proud of the interactive mirror. It shows the same person in two different ways, and when people drag it they can see the difference between a social media highlight and an ordinary day. It makes the main idea more memorable than just explaining it in a paragraph.

**4. Colour, layout, font and images:** I used a light paper background with black text for a magazine look. The red-orange colour makes the important words and choices stand out. Large bold writing catches attention, while the italic font makes some phrases feel more personal. The drawings help show the contrast between public and private moments.

## Part B — Multimodality

**5. Modes used:** I used written information, visual illustrations, statistics, layout and interactive/gestural modes through the slider, buttons and message choices. I also added optional speech narration and click sounds as aural elements.

**6. Most effective mode:** I think the visual and interactive modes are the most effective together. When someone moves the mirror divider, they can actually compare two versions of the same scene and understand why a post does not show everything.

**7. Written and visual elements together:** The pictures grab the reader's attention, while the written explanations make the meaning clear. For example, the mirror pictures show two different sides of the same person and the written question gets the audience to think about the comparison. One without the other would still communicate part of the idea but not as clearly.

**8. Removing one element:** If I removed the interactivity, the website would feel much more like a normal article. People could still learn from it, but they would not have the same chance to explore a message or see the effect of their choices.

## Part C — Critical Evaluation

**9. Engagement:** I think the website engages teenagers through the visual mirror, different feed lenses, two chat scenarios and the downloadable personal code. The website asks the audience to do something in each major part instead of only looking at pictures and reading text.

**10. Challenges:** One challenge was making the website detailed without making it confusing. The design separates the story into five clear chapters and uses the same colours and typography to keep it consistent. Another challenge was making the interactive elements work on smaller screens, which meant adjusting the layouts and testing the buttons and slider on mobile sizes. *Only keep this as a personal statement if you actually participated in or reviewed this work.*

**11. Three improvements with another lesson:** I would ask real students to try the site and record which sections were confusing, add more different fictional scenarios to the message lab, and record a custom narration instead of relying on the computer voice. These changes could make the website easier to use and more personal.
